About this project

Why Sustainable Development Goal 11, who this site is for, and where the numbers came from.

Abstract

This site was built to raise awareness of UN Sustainable Development Goal 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities. The goal was chosen because urbanization is no longer a distant trend — more than half the world's population already lives in cities, and that pressure is visible close to home. Port of Spain, Trinidad & Tobago's largest urban centre, experiences recurring flash flooding and traffic gridlock tied to ageing drainage infrastructure, which maps directly onto the housing, transport, and resilience targets that sit at the core of Goal 11.

Rather than treating "sustainable cities" as an abstract global statistic, this project pairs UN and UN-Habitat data with specific, sourced local context, so the case for action feels concrete rather than distant. The site covers four linked pressure points — housing, transport, green space, and resilience — and gives visitors a way to filter practical actions by the category they care about most.

Target audience

This site is aimed primarily at residents of Trinidad & Tobago, particularly young adults and students, who are more likely to encounter the daily effects of urban infrastructure strain — delayed commutes, flooded streets, limited green space — without necessarily connecting those experiences to a named global framework. A secondary audience is local civic groups and community advocates looking for a quick, sourced reference to cite when raising infrastructure concerns with local authorities.

A note on the data

Global figures are drawn from UN and UN-Habitat reporting current as of the 2025–2026 cycle. Local flooding details are summarized from repeated local news coverage rather than a single official dataset, and are presented as an illustrative pattern rather than a precise statistic.
