What actually moves the needle

Filter by category to see what's being done — and what you can push for — across transport, housing, green space, and waste.

Browse actions by category

Transport

Back reliable public routes

Support expansion and maintenance of bus and maxi-taxi routes so fewer trips depend on private cars — this is the single biggest lever on Target 11.2.

Transport

Push for safer walking routes

Advocate for sidewalks and crossings near schools and transport hubs — basic infrastructure that's often the first thing cut from budgets.

Housing

Support affordable-housing policy

Back zoning and financing reforms that make it easier to build housing people can actually afford, rather than only high-end units.

Housing

Formalize informal settlements

Push for upgrading programs — water, sanitation, land tenure — rather than displacement, for communities already living informally.

Green space

Protect existing green space

Oppose paving over parks and green corridors for parking or development — they're doing real cooling and drainage work, not just sitting there.

Green space

Plant and maintain street trees

Community tree-planting lowers urban heat and adds informal public space at very low cost.

Waste

Reduce single-use packaging

Lower-volume waste streams are easier for municipal systems to actually collect and manage properly.

Waste

Support proper drain maintenance

Waste dumped in waterways is a direct cause of the street flooding seen repeatedly in Port of Spain — clearing drains is a community-level action with an outsized effect.

Resilience

Know your flood risk

Check whether your route to work or school crosses a known flood point, and have a backup plan for heavy-rain days.

Resilience

Advocate for drainage upgrades

Raise ageing drainage infrastructure with local government representatives — it's the single most cited cause behind the capital's recurring flooding.

A few things people usually ask when they first hear about SDG 11.

No — Goal 11 applies at any city size. Port of Spain has about 478,000 people in its metro area, far smaller than a global mega-city, but it faces the same core issues: drainage infrastructure that hasn't kept pace with how the city is used, and transport corridors that back up during ordinary weather events.

Housing. UN-Habitat's 2026 report places adequate, affordable housing at the centre of the whole goal — it connects directly to transport need, informal settlement growth, and disaster vulnerability.

Local, specific pressure works better than general awareness: showing up to a community meeting about a known flood point, reporting a blocked drain, or using and defending public transport routes all have a measurable, local effect.
