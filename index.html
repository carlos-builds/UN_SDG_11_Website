Cities are where the future of sustainability gets decided.

More than half the world already lives in urban areas, and that share is still climbing. How we build, move through, and manage cities will shape whether development is inclusive, safe, and resilient — or whether it leaves people behind.

What SDG 11 actually asks for

Goal 11 of the United Nations' 2030 Agenda calls on countries to make cities and human settlements inclusive, safe, resilient, and sustainable. That means tackling housing, transport, waste, air quality, disaster resilience, and public space together — not as separate problems.

Why it's urgent

Urban growth is outpacing planning almost everywhere. Between 2000 and 2020, cities expanded their built-up land nearly four times faster than their population density increased — a clear sign of sprawl winning out over smart design. At the same time, informal settlements keep growing: over 1.16 billion people lived in slums or informal housing in 2024, more than 130 million above 2015 levels.

Why it's local too

Sustainable cities isn't only a problem for mega-cities abroad. In Trinidad & Tobago, recurring flash flooding in Port of Spain — on streets like Wrightson Road and Independence Square — regularly brings traffic to a standstill and disrupts daily life, a pattern local officials have linked to ageing drainage infrastructure and clogged waterways. It's the same global story — infrastructure struggling to keep pace with how a city actually gets used — playing out at home.

Four pressure points, one goal

SDG 11 covers a wide mandate. These are the themes this site focuses on.

Housing

Affordable, adequate housing is the foundation Goal 11 is built on — and the area furthest off track.

Transport

Convenient, safe public transport determines who can actually reach jobs, school, and services.

Green & public space

Parks and open space aren't decoration — they're tied to health, cooling, and community life.

Resilience

Flooding, storms, and heat hit dense urban areas hardest, especially where drainage and planning lag behind.
